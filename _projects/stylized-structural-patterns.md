At Disney Research Studios Zurich, I contributed to a research direction on synthetic and semi-synthetic data for pretraining neural networks and diffusion models.

The project studies a practical constraint in computer vision: large real-image datasets are expensive to curate and can raise privacy, licensing, and legal concerns, while purely synthetic datasets often leave a domain gap. The work combines improved neural fractals with reverse stylization, transferring visual features from a small license-free real-image set onto synthetic images.

My work focused on diffusion-related experiments, evaluation design, and attention-map similarity as one way to compare synthetic and real-data pretraining behavior.
